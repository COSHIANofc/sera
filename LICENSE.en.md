## Article 6 — Providing Corresponding Source

### 6.1 Obligation to Provide

When distributing a Covered Work in Executable Form to a third party, the distributor must provide, or make obtainable to the recipient, the source code of the CDL-covered portions corresponding to that Executable Form.

This obligation applies whether the Covered Work is unmodified or modified.

The obligation under this Article does not categorically require providing source code for independent works.

### 6.2 Scope of Provision

Corresponding Source includes, according to the nature of the Covered Work:

1. The complete Source Form of CDL-covered files corresponding to the distributed Executable Form.
2. Changes and additions made to those files.
3. Scripts, configurations, and other related files reasonably necessary to edit, modify, build, or generate the CDL-covered portions.
4. Necessary dependencies and their version information.
5. Reasonable editing, build, or generation instructions.

The Source Form provided must be the form preferred for creating and modifying the Covered Work.

If an editable original form exists, providing only the following does not satisfy this Article:

1. Obfuscated code.
2. Compiled code.
3. Code generated by decompilation.
4. Merely reformatted converted code.
5. Artifacts generated from editable original data that are unsuitable for editing.
6. Source Form missing files necessary for modification or generation.

For technical materials, documentation, configuration files, data files, and other non-software Covered Works, provide CDL-covered editable original data used to create or modify the distributed artifact as well.

However, if the distributed form itself is the form preferred for modifying the work, there is no obligation to newly create a separate Source Form.

Deliberately deleting or concealing Source Form does not avoid an otherwise applicable provision obligation.

For independent third-party libraries, development tools, and editing tools, providing appropriate sources of acquisition, versions, and other necessary information may substitute for providing copies.

This Article does not require providing Source Form of independent works or disclosing independent nonpublic code to reproduce the entire Combined Work.

### 6.3 Methods of Provision and Notice to Recipients

Corresponding Source may be provided by any of the following methods:

1. Providing it with the Executable Form.
2. Identifying its source of acquisition on the distribution page or in the distribution.
3. Identifying the corresponding public repository and a specific version, tag, or commit.

Regardless of the method used, the distributor must notify recipients of the Executable Form, in a reasonably ascertainable manner, how to obtain Corresponding Source and which version of CDL applies to it.

Notice may be given on the distribution page, in a README, NOTICE, license information, statements in the distribution, or another appropriate manner.

Recipients must be able to identify a valid means of obtaining Corresponding Source when they receive the Executable Form.

The provided source must correspond to the Executable Form actually distributed.

Merely linking to the latest source does not satisfy this Article if the correspondence to the distributed Executable Form cannot be verified.

When an online source is specified, the distributor must maintain the availability of Corresponding Source during the period of distribution of that Executable Form.

When the Executable Form and complete Corresponding Source are provided together, no additional period of provision after distribution ends is required.

This Article does not categorically require opening a publicly accessible repository. It is sufficient that recipients of the Executable Form can obtain Corresponding Source in accordance with this License.

### 6.4 Terms and Costs

Corresponding Source must be provided so recipients can use it under CDL.

For electronic provision of Corresponding Source, the distributor must not charge recipients additional fees.

Prohibited additional fees include license fees, download fees, handling fees, and other fees charged as a condition of obtaining Corresponding Source.

For provision on physical media, reasonable media and shipping costs may be charged.

1. Private keys.
2. Passwords and authentication credentials.
3. API keys and access tokens.
4. Personal information.
5. Other confidential information that is not itself Corresponding Source.

The mere presence of confidential information does not permit withholding all or necessary portions of Corresponding Source that must otherwise be provided.

Merely designating CDL-covered code as a trade secret, corporate secret, or other confidential information does not avoid this Article's provision obligations.
